Professional profile

I work at the intersection of identity, enterprise IT and service assurance. My experience has been built inside international, regulated environments where access control, availability and incident discipline are everyday requirements, not abstractions.

My current role focuses on service assurance and critical operations: 24/7 monitoring, L1/L2 incident management, major incident coordination, root cause analysis and continuous improvement, under compliance frameworks including DORA, FCA and PRA.

Previously, I spent three years at a global enterprise supporting Microsoft identity and endpoint technologies at Tier 2 level, with direct, hands-on responsibility for Entra ID, Intune, Autopilot, Active Directory, IAM and Secure Access Workstations for 500+ users.

I am building on that operational foundation toward a specialization in Identity & Access Security and Cloud Security, through structured certification (SC-900, AZ-500, SC-300) and hands-on lab projects documented in the section below.

I follow recognized voices in the security community, including Chema Alonso's work on identity, authentication and digital risk, to help shape how I think about the field. I do not currently hold offensive-security or penetration-testing skills; my background is in identity administration and enterprise operations, and every specialization claimed on this page is scoped to that experience.

How I approach technology

  1. 01

    Understand the service

    Before troubleshooting infrastructure, understand the service, its dependencies, its users and its business impact.

  2. 02

    Protect identity and access

    Identity, authentication and authorization are fundamental security boundaries across modern enterprise environments.

  3. 03

    Observe the environment

    Monitoring and useful operational visibility allow teams to detect degradation before it becomes a larger business impact.

  4. 04

    Respond systematically

    Effective incident management requires prioritization, communication, escalation and technical discipline.

  5. 05

    Learn from failure

    Root Cause Analysis and post-incident reviews should lead to measurable improvements rather than simply closing tickets.

  6. 06

    Automate what can be repeated

    Repetitive operational work is an opportunity for automation, consistency and better engineering practices.
